PROTOTYPE

Goalie posing: is it believable?

Each Stance fixes the torso and legs. You drag the Glove and Blocker Hands. Each arm bends to reach its hand, and the hand stops at the edge of its reach box. The stick stays in the Blocker Hand with its blade on the ice, so the blade slides forward or back as the hand moves. It only lifts off the ice when the shaft is too short to reach down. Question: does this look and behave believably enough, and what reach box (clamp range) does each Stance need?

Walkthroughs

Hand limits for this Stance

These are the app's guard rails: the furthest each hand can be moved or turned before it stops. They're drawn as the orange and blue boxes. Positions are in cm at the current height and scale with height; wrist angles are in degrees. They should be loose enough for every pose you'd teach and tight enough to stop impossible ones. You don't need to tune them; just tell me when one gets in your way or lets something silly through.

Things to judge

  • Do the three Stances read as Stand-up, Butterfly and RVH from Shooter's view?
  • RVH: the post-side pad is down like a butterfly pad and locked against the post, the far leg spreads wide with its skate planted out to the side, the goalie sits over the post knee leaning so the post-side shoulder seals the post, the post-side hand seals beside the hip, and the body is turned 30° toward the post. Does it read right from behind the net and from the shooter?
  • Stick: the blade now lies in the same plane as the paddle. Do blade, hands and head line up roughly, blade first?
  • Wrists: select a hand and press Rotate, or use the Turn / Tilt / Twist sliders. Are the default limits sensible?
  • Kid sizes (1.2 m): do the proportions still pass?
